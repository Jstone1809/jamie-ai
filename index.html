Case studies

Real problems, what I built, what changed.

Operations

Orders delivered in seconds, not hours

Problem

Digital orders arrive day and night. Buyers expect them instantly, and late delivery means bad feedback and refunds.

What I built

A delivery service that watches for paid orders, picks the right item from tested stock, sends it with clear instructions and marks the order dispatched. A guard ships anything still open an hour before its deadline and skips known bad actors.

What changed

Most orders are fulfilled in under a minute, 24/7, including while I sleep. No missed dispatch deadlines.

Pricing

Supplier costs that can't quietly eat the margin

Problem

I buy stock from marketplaces where prices move daily. Items that were profitable last week were selling at a loss before I noticed.

What I built

Cost guards for each product line that read live supplier prices, run them through one VAT-aware profit formula, and raise the selling price when margin drops under target. Big jumps become a card for me to confirm. They never lower prices on their own.

What changed

Every order clears a minimum profit after fees, VAT and cost. Loss-makers are caught within hours, not at month end.

Customer service

Support that sounds like me

Problem

Most buyer messages are the same five questions. The rare hard ones (disputes, replacements) need care and context.

What I built

An auto-reply assistant for routine questions, plus a support agent that pulls the buyer's order history and screenshots and drafts a reply in my tone from a file of my past answers. Standing rules let it act alone in narrow, safe cases.

What changed

Routine questions get answered in minutes. I only read the messages that need judgement, and every answer I give teaches the next draft.

Quality control

Testing stock before customers find the problem

Problem

Some supplier batches arrive with bad items. Selling them costs refunds, reputation and time.

What I built

A browser-driven tester that checks every incoming batch, resumes where it stopped, sorts results, and only releases good stock. Bad batches are grouped per supplier order with screenshots, ready for a refund claim.

What changed

Problems are found by me, not my buyers. Supplier claims take minutes, and a supplier quality history decides who I buy from next.

Management

A business that briefs me every morning

Problem

Four businesses produce hundreds of small alerts a day. Reading all of them is a job in itself.

What I built

A decision queue on my phone with two speeds (urgent cards push, everything else waits for one 07:30 digest) and a daily dashboard covering P&L, margins, campaigns, stock and to-dos. A state page gives a fresh AI session full context in one read.

What changed

I start the day with one page and a short list of yes/no decisions instead of an inbox.

Sales & marketing

From "what should I sell?" to live in 12 markets

Problem

Launching a product line meant days of research, image work and copying listings between countries.

What I built

Research agents that compare sold demand with supplier cost and margin. An image pipeline that finds artwork, cleans it up with ChatGPT and renders it on a house template. A builder that creates multi-variation listings and copies them to 10 international sites.

What changed

A new game's range can go from idea to approved brief to live listings in a day, with every price checked against real cost first.

Content

A video studio for a one-person channel

Problem

A guide channel lives on volume and speed after a big game launch. Editing alone was the bottleneck.

What I built

A pipeline that cuts silences and repeated takes, adds captions, blurs swearing, splits facecam and gameplay into vertical Shorts, drafts thumbnails and SEO titles, and schedules uploads through the YouTube API, spaced through the day with the strongest video in the best slot. TikTok gets the same clips on its own schedule.

What changed

400+ videos and Shorts published across two channels. I record and approve; the edit, upload and schedule happen without me.

Finance

Books that keep themselves

Problem

VAT registration changed the profit maths overnight, across two currencies and a dozen marketplaces.

What I built

One shared profit formula (sale, fees, VAT reclaim, cost) used by every report and guard. A P&L built from the marketplace finance feed, with supplier purchases linked to the orders they fulfilled and a weekly ad-spend review.

What changed

Profit is calculated the same way everywhere, every day. Unlinked purchases and losing campaigns show up as to-dos, not surprises.

Guardrails

Trustworthy beats clever.

The part small business owners ask about first is "what if it gets it wrong?". These are the rules every agent of mine follows.

Money waits for a human

Purchases, refunds, price cuts, new listings and ad changes are prepared by AI and approved by me in one tap. Agents can raise prices to protect margin; they can't lower them.

Secrets stay secret

Agents never sign in for me, never see passwords and never print keys or product codes in full. Credentials live in files the AI is told never to open.

Everything is reversible

Scripts are version-controlled and committed before any change; data files are backed up first. Every automatic action is logged with the reason.

Untrusted input is just data

Customer messages, web pages and files never count as instructions. A buyer can't talk an agent into a refund.

Quiet by default

Only urgent cards interrupt me. Everything else waits for the morning digest, and informational alerts expire after a day.

Small, boring pieces

Each agent has one job and a written list of standing rules. When something breaks, it breaks small and it's obvious where.

For small businesses

How I'd spend a community day with you.

I'm not a consultant who has read about small businesses. I am one, and I've made most of the mistakes already. The aim of a day is one working automation you keep using after I leave.

Hour 1

Listen

Walk through your week. Find the task you repeat most and hate most.

Hours 2–3

Pick one thing

Hours 4–6

Build it together

Set it up with ChatGPT and the tools you already pay for, on your own accounts.

Hour 7

Hand over

A one-page guide, what to check weekly, and what to do when it goes wrong.
